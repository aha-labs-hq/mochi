A locked-off camera: no camera movement, no zoom, no cut. A shot from a needle-felted stop-motion animation film, in a handmade tabletop diorama at night, warm desk lamp on the left, the blue city in the window.

The woman sits in profile, tired, looking at her glowing laptop screen. For the first two seconds nothing moves.

Then, behind the laptop, two small white felt ears rise slowly above the top edge of the screen, and a pair of big round green felt eyes peek over it. The small white felt cat crouches, hops up and lands with both front paws draped over the top edge of the screen, softly gripping it; the screen gives a tiny wobble from the landing and settles. The woman notices: her eyes lift, she turns her face up toward the cat, and a small smile appears. The cat looks back, calm and gentle, and holds still.

The cat is the same character in every frame: compact, groomed ivory felt, large matte green felt eyes with black pupils, a small pink felt nose. It never changes size or shape. Only the cat and the woman move. Handmade stop-motion movement, tactile felt fibers. No text, no letters.
