### Consolidated direction — transparency, anatomy and continuity

These are cumulative user requirements, not interchangeable style suggestions:

1. **The desktop is real.** Opening pixels reveal the user's actual desktop beneath the full-screen dimmer specified in [entrance.md](entrance.md); a pale panel, captured wallpaper, checkerboard or clear canvas over an opaque WebView does not qualify. A screen-sized transparent native stage is allowed; it is not an opaque full-screen takeover. The character is initially small at the physical bottom edge. The felt frame emerges as part of its landing, without an upfront invitation or click gate.
2. **Mochi is a complete animal in a resting pose.** The user explicitly clarified that the root shows Mochi lying/resting with forepaws at the edge; arms, torso and hind legs are merely obscured by pose and framing. Its two-dimensional visible silhouette is not an anatomical blueprint. First reconstruct the complete resting animal, with its torso extending behind the head and forepaws, rather than extending the front crop downward. On open desktop space, show the body and limbs appropriate to the pose. A half-body crop is valid only with a spatially plausible occluder, never as a severed sticker floating in empty space.
3. **Identity is not a frozen pose.** Preserve the recognizable face, ears, eye/nose geometry, matte felt eyes, ivory color and dense fine fibers while permitting anatomically coherent limbs, joint movement and new views. The first full-body study changed the identity into a generic rounded plush toy; the next wrongly extruded the original crop into a long capsule. Both are rejected. Do not infer that Mochi lacks a neck, arms or legs merely because they are hidden in the original pose. Full-body studies remain unapproved and never replace the canonical root in place.
4. **Peek is acting.** Ears, gaze, paws, head and body must perform a coherent, physically understandable entrance. A still translated upward, face warped by a shader or unrelated idle cycle is not a peek. The complete-body identity and action must pass source review before extraction; the approved storyboard alone cannot approve a generated video. Rejected half-body studies and subsequent event experiments remain outside autonomous playback; the manifest records their actual provenance and rejection reasons.
5. **Paper is a material, not anatomy.** The user allows a warm-white sticker rim and restrained paper curl, including Three.js. That permission does not approve arbitrary deformation, a rigid sliding card, a jointed doll or use of Three.js everywhere. Paper geometry may bend; the underlying face and limbs must remain video-owned. White paper and blue matte contamination are different things.
6. **Size is optical, not canvas size.** Measure the visible character bounds in logical display points. The earlier 180–200-point visible-width proposal is a starting experiment, not an accepted constant or permission to scale a severed body. A full body may need a different footprint. Consider screen size, Dock position, tail/paw clearance and perceived distance; use a smaller supporting presence during authorization. Do not couple character scale directly to animated native window dimensions.
7. **Narrative earns attention.** Demonstrate how 麻薯 makes a team's GitHub information understandable in the places they talk. Let the same question, sources and answer acquire meaning through motion; do not substitute a feature slideshow or anthropomorphic logo animation. The introduction is skippable; provider authorization remains explicit and resumable, with no invented progress or background writes.
8. **Continuity is perceptual and technical.** Preserve scene identity and coordinate continuity through expansion and contraction. Native focus, chrome and hit testing are part of the handoff. A single NSWindow with discontinuous content is not better than a correctly synchronized handoff. Evidence from [the inspected Dia package](dia-onboarding-evidence.md) informs this boundary without claiming recovered source or current timings.

Acceptance order: approved storyboard → genuine source with consistent full-body anatomy → canonical matte and seam gates → isolated native preview → actual-scale continuous encounter and handoff. ### Characters have different work, not just different appearances

The user clarified on September 7, 2026 that **麻薯 is the first character, specializing in GitHub as a team's product-information center**. Future characters may serve other situations, including creative work; each such promise needs its own product contract. The product-family name and whether future capability-specific characters ship together or separately are undecided; do not rename the App or introduce a character marketplace on that assumption.

- Distinguish the shared experience from each character's promise. Common principles are understandable permissions, continuity, recovery, accessibility and a truthful first result. A character supplies its own personality, visual language, example, relevant connections and useful outcome. ChatGPT → Feishu → GitHub is the present character's integration route, not a mandatory family-wide onboarding sequence.

A successful connection-test reply proves transport, not the value of a product-information center. Do not imply autonomous roadmap decisions, code changes or unrequested Issue writes.

### The decisions

1. **A first meeting gets its own space.** Begin with the transparent performance described above, not an authorization-sized rectangle or invitation gate. The scene unfolds continuously and remains skippable; authorization then uses a spacious normal window constrained to the available screen. Reflow at large text sizes and on small displays; keep the primary action visible. No inner sheet, giant phone layout or QR squeezed beneath the cat. The same App owns the transition into the smaller daily companion; do not invent another installer or application.
2. **Three chapters, not a service checklist: meet, connect, work together.** Chapters describe intent, not an exact number of pages or clicks. Every screen has one clear next action and a visible way back. Explain required accounts before sending the user through authorization; do not reveal a surprise subscription or organization-approval requirement at the last step.
3. **Connection proof is not the first useful result.** Once thinking, the Bot and a user-chosen group are genuinely ready, a real exchange can prove that they can reach each other. For 麻薯's product-information role, the meaningful payoff is an answer grounded in a user-selected GitHub project; do not present a generic greeting as fulfillment of that promise. Missing GitHub cannot masquerade as a broken Feishu connection. Reorder connections around the chosen first task, preserving explicit authorization. Partial-readiness behavior is proposed, not a claim about the current all-connections gate.
4. **Onboarding earns its ending.** An acknowledged scan or successful login is not the finale. End with a real, useful result requested by the user, or honestly offer to continue later. A read-only summary of one selected project's open work is a suitable first project task. Do not silently create an Issue, send a group message or invent an assistant response to manufacture delight.
5. **Settings are a workbench, not a replay of the introduction.** Accounts, Feishu/groups and GitHub/projects remain directly reachable. Show identity, access and one relevant action per connection. Re-enter only an unfinished or deliberately changed connection; preserve other completed work. Reuse domain actions, validators and custody, not necessarily the entire screen component.
