A locked-off camera: no camera movement, no zoom, no cut. A close shot from a needle-felted stop-motion animation film, in a handmade tabletop office diorama late at night: a warm brass desk lamp on the right, the blue city in the window behind her.

She has sent a message and is waiting for an answer that does not come. Her chin rests on her hand. For a moment she is still. Then her eyes slowly lift up and to the left, as if to a clock on the wall, hold there, and come back down to the screen. She lets out a small sigh: her shoulders rise and sink, and her cheek settles a little deeper into her hand. Behind her, one of the warm lit windows in the building across the street goes dark.

She is the same felt puppet in every frame: white felt sweater, blue felt collar, cream felt headphones, curly black wool hair, a long felt nose, heavy tired eyes. Only the woman and that one distant window change. Each puppet and object stays exactly the same in every frame: same shape, size, colours and felt. Handmade stop-motion movement, tactile needle-felt fibers, fine fuzz, soft shallow depth of field. No text, no letters, no numbers; any screen shows only a soft glow.
