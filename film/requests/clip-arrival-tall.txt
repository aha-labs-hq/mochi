A locked-off vertical shot: no camera movement, no zoom, no cut. A shot from a needle-felted stop-motion animation film, in a handmade tabletop diorama at night: a glowing laptop on a desk, a warm desk lamp at the left edge, the blue city in the window behind.

For the first half second nothing moves. Then, behind the laptop, two small white felt ears rise slowly above the top edge of the screen, and a pair of big round green felt eyes peek over it. The small white felt cat crouches, makes a small hop and lands with both front paws draped over the top edge of the screen, softly gripping it; the screen gives a tiny wobble and settles. The cat looks down and to the right, calm and gentle, and holds still. The cat stays fully inside the frame the whole time.

The cat is the same character in every frame: compact, groomed ivory felt, large matte green felt eyes with black pupils, a small pink felt nose. It never changes size or shape. Only the cat moves. Handmade stop-motion movement, tactile felt fibers. No text, no letters.
