A locked-off camera: no camera movement, no zoom, no cut. A shot from a needle-felted stop-motion animation film, in a handmade tabletop office diorama late at night: a warm brass desk lamp on the right, the blue city in the big window, the rest of the office dark and empty.

The tired woman with curly black wool hair and cream felt headphones rests her chin on her hand, looking at her laptop. She takes a small breath, lifts her chin off her hand and starts typing with both hands, in short bursts. She stops with one finger hovering over the keys, unsure, then types again, and pauses to read what she wrote, leaning in a little toward the screen. Far behind her, in the building across the street, one lit window goes dark.

She is the same felt puppet in every frame: white felt sweater, blue felt collar, cream felt headphones, curly black wool hair, a long felt nose. Only the woman and that one distant window change. Each puppet and object stays exactly the same in every frame: same shape, size, colours and felt. Handmade stop-motion movement, tactile needle-felt fibers, fine fuzz, soft shallow depth of field. No text, no letters, no numbers; any screen shows only a soft glow.
