A locked-off camera: no camera movement, no zoom, no cut. A shot from a needle-felted stop-motion animation film, in a handmade tabletop office diorama by day, sunlight through the big window.

The boxy pale green felt engineer with big cream felt headphones types on his laptop in deep concentration. A felt hand reaching in from the left taps his shoulder with one finger: tap. A pause. Tap, tap. Tap. With each tap one more small blank felt speech bubble pops into the air around his head, and all the bubbles bob gently, crowding him. He keeps typing and never takes off his headphones. On the last tap his fingers stop, and his big round eyes slide sideways toward the finger, weary.

He is the same felt puppet in every frame: a boxy pale green felt head, big round white eyes with black pupils, a long felt nose, a flat felt mouth, a red felt knob on the side of his head, cream felt headphones. The speech bubbles are plain, blank felt. Each puppet and object stays exactly the same in every frame: same shape, size, colours and felt. Handmade stop-motion movement, tactile needle-felt fibers, fine fuzz, soft shallow depth of field. No text, no letters, no numbers; any screen shows only a soft glow.
